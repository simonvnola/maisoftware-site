Beyond Work: A Basic Income for Australia's AI Transition

Calculator

Use this calculator to see what the paper's proposal would mean for your household and for the Commonwealth budget: Stage 1, a targeted floor, and a conditional pathway to a basic income if artificial intelligence (AI) displaces work. Every result is a modelled estimate from the paper's own model, run in your browser; it is not advice or a forecast.

Model of 21 October 2026 (Part F4). Dollar figures are A$ nominal; years are financial years, so 2028-29 runs from 1 July 2028 to 30 June 2029. Nothing you enter is stored or sent anywhere.

What happens to me

Enter your household's details to see the change to its income in the first year of Stage 1, 2028-29. Simple mode uses eight questions; Detailed mode adds your age, each child's age and every lever in the model.

This version models Stage 1 as the paper's 22 October design: the floor and the child payment, with no dividend. The recommended design also drops the child payment's middle-income supplement and raises Rent Assistance by 75%; it will be added when its full costing has been checked.

The model’s levers

Every lever in the paper’s model, grouped by the section of the paper it comes from. Each shows its recommended setting: the paper’s, with no dividend.

Part 2 (the dividend): options described, none recommended

From chapter 5, The proposal (Part 2: options for Parliament).

Stage 1, a targeted floor: rates and protections

From chapter 5, The proposal (Rates and indexation; The no-worse-off rule).

Caseload response to the floor

How many more people stay on, or return to, payments once the floor is lifted. Recommended setting: With re-entry (0.39).

What is this?

e61’s job-finding effect is 0.21. The other settings add 25% or 50% of the extra job separations re-entering payment, a share the paper assumes. Students and Parenting Payment recipients respond at the job-finding effect in every case.

Keeps current recipients’ payments from falling under today’s rules, through offsets, a 50 cent phase-out and a family top-up. Recommended setting: on.

What is this?

The Recipient Protection Offset is capped at the tax current rules levy on the payment; the offset on other income, the 50 cent phase-out and the family top-up come with it. It protects the payment, not income after rent: a household whose rent rises with its gain can still end up worse off after housing costs.

New permanent residents wait before they get the dividend and the child payment. Recommended setting: on.

What is this?

Four years for the dividend, as for JobSeeker Payment, and one year for the child payment, as for Family Tax Benefit: about 518,000 adults and 55,500 children in 2028-29.

Coverage

Who can receive the dividend. Recommended setting: Citizens and permanent residents.

80%

The share of adults without a tax return who sign up for the dividend.

Unit: % of about 1.5 million eligible adults who lodge no return and get no payment. Recommended setting: 80%. Reference points: 92% reached by the first US stimulus payment in 2020.

What is this?

Lodgers are paid once a year at assessment; the rest must enrol with Services Australia. The rate per adult is set on everyone eligible, and what goes unclaimed stays in the dividend account. No figure exists for an Australian payment that needs enrolment, so 80% is an assumption.

The dividend and the fund

From chapter 6, Making the dollars work (The central path with the wealth-tax fund).

The fund’s return

What the Permanent AI Dividend Fund earns each year. Recommended setting: CPI + 4%.

What is this?

CPI + 4% is the Future Fund mandate’s lower bound. At the bond rate the fund earns no risk premium, so it grows more slowly and pays less.

4.00%

The share of the fund paid out as the dividend each year.

Unit: % of the fund’s average balance over the last three years, paid out each year. Recommended setting: 4.00%.

What is this?

At 4% the dividend spends the fund’s expected real return, so its real value lasts. A higher rate pays more now, and the fund’s real value falls.

0%

Part of last year’s excess profits tax added to the dividend.

Unit: % of last year’s excess profits tax, added to the dividend. Recommended setting: 0%.

What is this?

Applies only with a dividend fund, options (i) and (v). The paper’s rule takes the largest whole per cent at which the other taxes still pay for the floor, the child payment, the protections and the income-fall review to 2040-41; with the wealth-tax fund no share meets it, since a levy is needed from 2039-40 even at 0%. A higher share raises the dividend and brings the levy forward.

Wealth tax: further losses in other taxes

Revenue the wealth tax might cost beyond the income tax the PBO has costed. Recommended setting: None.

What is this?

The PBO warns the wealth tax could reduce other revenue, without an estimate. The fund receives the tax net of these losses too, so they shrink the dividend, and the levied top-ups that keep it from being cut grow.

Stages 2 and 3: a conditional pathway

From chapter 6, Making the dollars work (Triggers for Stages 2 and 3 (chapter 5); Funding if Stages 2 and 3 are triggered).

If triggered, cost them on

The AI displacement the later stages are costed on, if they are triggered. Recommended setting: Smallest shock that meets the test.

What is this?

The stages start only if AI displaces work. Costed on the smallest displacement that meets the need test, they need income-tax rates 15.6 and 30.9 points higher; with no displacement, 14.9 and 25.8. The displacement lever, if set, takes over.

When the taxes fall short

What happens when the taxes do not cover the cost. Recommended setting: Levy sized to the gap.

What is this?

Levy: a levy on market income set each year to the gap and never above it (with a dividend fund, including the top-ups that keep the dividend from being cut). At the recommended settings Stage 1 needs one from 2044-45. Borrow: the gaps add to debt instead.

AI receipts and an AI displacement shock

From chapter 6, Making the dollars work (AI receipts and the dividend; An AI displacement shock).

A$0bn a year

Public spending on AI each year.

Unit: A$bn a year from 2027-28, counted as a budget cost. Recommended setting: A$0bn a year.

0.05 points

Extra productivity growth from each A$10 billion a year of public AI investment.

Unit: percentage points of growth a year. Recommended setting: 0.05 points.

What is this?

Your assumption: no Australian estimate exists. The extra receipts count against the investment’s cost.

Macro feedback in a displacement

What happens to GDP and profits when wages are lost in a displacement. Recommended setting: GDP held.

What is this?

Applies when the displacement lever is set. Whether the feedback raises or lowers the cost is not known: if GDP falls with the lost wages the levy is higher; if the lost wages reappear as taxed company profits it is lower.

100%

How much of the PBO’s costing the excess profits tax raises.

Unit: % of the PBO costing (ECR-2025-3194). Recommended setting: 100%.

The economy and population: sensitivity assumptions

From chapter 6, Making the dollars work (Sensitivity; Assumptions).

225,000 a year

People added to the population each year.

Unit: people a year from 2027-28. Recommended setting: 225,000 a year. Reference points: 225,000 ABS medium; 292,100 in the year to March 2026.

What is this?

Migrants have the population’s age and visa mix (the paper’s assumption). New residents serve the waiting periods first, so extra arrivals add receipts before they add cost.

3.5% a year

Growth in wages each year; it also indexes the floor.

Unit: Wage Price Index, % a year. Recommended setting: 3.5% a year. Reference points: 3.5% Budget.

2.5% a year

Growth in consumer prices each year.

Unit: CPI, % a year; also sets the fund’s CPI-linked return. Recommended setting: 2.5% a year. Reference points: 2.5% Budget.

50 basis points

How far the cash rate rises for each 1% of GDP of extra demand.

Unit: basis points per 1% of GDP of extra demand. Recommended setting: 50 basis points. Reference points: 25 low; 50 central; 75 high.

What is this?

Range from the paper’s household model. Feeds the mortgage line in the household results.

Fuel and minerals revenue after the PBO window

How two revenue measures grow after the PBO costing window ends in 2038-39. Recommended setting: Flat in real terms.

What is this?

The fuel-tax-credit and coal-and-minerals measures after 2038-39. The paper holds them flat in real terms, since machinery keeps its credits while light vehicles electrify; growing with GDP is its high case.

100

Scales the minerals and gas measures up or down.

Unit: index, 100 = PBO costings; scales the minerals and gas measures. Recommended setting: 100.

0.16

Earnings given up for each dollar of dividend.

Unit: cents of earnings given up per dollar of dividend; parents’ response to the child payment stays at 16 cents. Recommended setting: 0.16. Reference points: 0.16 OpenResearch trial; 0.29 household model.

19.5%

The share of renters’ gains that landlords take in higher rent.

Unit: % of renters’ gains taken in higher rent. Recommended setting: 19.5%. Reference points: 6.6% AHURI low; 32.4% AHURI high.

What is this?

The paper’s setting is the midpoint of the AHURI range. Feeds the rent line in the household results.

Main levers

Eight settings that move the figures most. Each shows its recommended setting.

A dividend for every adult

Whether to add one of the four dividend designs the paper describes. It recommends none of them now. Recommended setting: No dividend (the default).

What is this?

Option (i): a wealth tax builds a permanent fund and the fund’s payout is the dividend. Option (iv): the same dividend paid each year by the other taxes, with no wealth tax and no fund. Option (v): a fund built only from the other taxes’ surpluses, so a smaller dividend that starts later. Each adds a payment of a few hundred dollars a year at most by 2045-46 and machinery to run it. The Stage 1 figures change with this choice; Stages 2 and 3 are costed the same way whatever you choose here.

A$569.81 a week

The rate JobSeeker, Youth Allowance, student and Parenting payments are lifted to.

Unit: A$ a week, March quarter 2026, indexed to wages. Recommended setting: A$569.81 a week. Reference points: JobSeeker single rate A$412 a week from 20 September 2026, A$416.85 with the Energy Supplement; poverty line A$569.81 a week, Melbourne Institute, March quarter 2026; ACOSS proposal A$600 a week.

What is this?

The poverty line is the Melbourne Institute line for a single person not in the workforce. Switch the floor off to model Stage 1 without it.

100%

What the six revenue measures raise, as a share of the PBO costings.

Unit: % of the PBO costings. Recommended setting: 100%. Reference points: 70% downside.

0.20

How much taxable income falls when tax rates rise.

Unit: elasticity of taxable income; also used for the child-payment taper. Recommended setting: 0.20. Reference points: 0.2 PBO; 0.4 stress test.

1.2% a year

How fast output per hour worked grows each year.

Unit: % a year, sustained. Recommended setting: 1.2% a year. Reference points: 1.2% Budget; 1.6% Productivity Commission; 1.5% to 2.0% Intergenerational Report AI upside.

What is this?

Faster growth raises the excess profits tax in proportion, and with it the dividend and the fund (the paper’s Table F45 method).

0.0 points

How many adults lose work to AI between 2032-33 and 2035-36.

AI displacement: fall in employment scenario

Unit: percentage points of adults, phased in from 2032-33 to 2035-36. Recommended setting: 0.0 points. Reference points: 3 moderate; 8 severe.

What is this?

The paper’s scenarios: wages, JobSeeker numbers and company profits move with employment. A displacement is declared once the need test has been met for eight quarters. The reserve, then a loan from the fund, bridge the gap until the first year Stage 2 could start, when the levy starts repaying the loan. With no fund, the surpluses the other taxes have kept meet a gap first, then the levy.

4.5%

The share of the labour force out of work from 2028-29.

Unit: % of the labour force, from 2028-29. Recommended setting: 4.5%. Reference points: 4.5% Budget 2026-27 forecast; 4.6% ABS Labour Force, August 2026.

What is this?

JobSeeker numbers move in proportion.

Conditional pathway

Whether the year-by-year charts show Stages 2 and 3 starting. Stage 1 figures do not change with this choice. Recommended setting: Stage 1 continuing.

What is this?

Stage 1 continuing is the central path. The universal stages are a conditional pathway: the tests are legislated (the AI need test, a randomised trial and a Parliamentary Budget Office certificate), no rates are. Stage 2 would pay the JobSeeker rate to every adult; Stage 3, which the paper describes but does not authorise, the Age Pension rate from 2037-38. Trigger-timed starts Stage 2 when youth unemployment trips the trigger. The model costs Stages 2 and 3 only with the paper’s wealth-tax fund, so the charts use it whenever this pathway includes them.

    What it is

    The paper's fiscal model, rewritten in JavaScript and run in your browser. The source files are the paper's supplementary files ubi_model.py, partc.py, partd.py, partE.py, partF.py and partF_r8.py to partF_r13b.py (model of 21 October 2026, Part F4).

    Each year it costs the payment, the payments it replaces, the tax changes, the floor and its caseload response, the protections and the child payment over 2,001 points of the ATO income distribution. It runs the Permanent AI Dividend Fund year by year. It tracks the cash balance, net debt and net financial worth against the Budget baseline, with the fund treated as the Future Fund is.

    How Part 2 (the dividend) works in each option

    The paper describes four Part 2 options and recommends none of them now; the calculator's default is no dividend. In option (i) the fund receives the wealth tax net of the income tax it costs, which the PBO's interaction costing (ECR-2025-3901) gives year by year. That loss keeps growing with GDP after the tax's last year, 2038-39, and the fund bears it. The fund also receives any share of the excess profits tax the Act sets, and the surpluses the other taxes leave after the floor, the child payment and the protections.

    Each year the fund pays out 4% of its average balance over the last three years, a year in arrears; 10% goes to a reserve. The dividend is not cut in cash terms while a levied top-up can keep it whole. In the first year that top-up would pass a quarter of the dividend, the guarantee lapses: the dividend follows the payout and the loss is charged to the fund's capital until the fund runs out.

    Option (iv) pays the same dividend from the other taxes, with no wealth tax and no fund. Option (v) builds a fund from the other taxes' surpluses alone, under the same rules. With no option, Stage 1 stands alone. The dividend is paid once a year, at tax assessment, or after enrolment for adults who lodge no return (80% of them assumed to enrol). No income test counts it.

    When the other taxes fall short, a levy on market income sized to the gap meets it. A displacement is declared once the need test has been met for eight quarters. From then the reserve, then a loan from the fund at the bond rate (up to a quarter of its balance), bridge the gap until the first year Stage 2 could start. The levy then repays the loan over ten years, whether or not a vote is held. With no fund there is no loan: the surpluses the other taxes have kept meet a gap first, then the levy. Stages 2 and 3 are a conditional pathway: the tests are legislated, no rates are, and Stage 3 is described, not authorised. The model costs each with one rate rise, large enough under both forms of the taxpayer response, and only with the wealth-tax fund of option (i), so the calculator costs them that way whatever dividend you choose. Household poverty is not recomputed live: Stage 1 is interpolated between runs of the paper's household model, and Stages 2 and 3 use the paper's figures.

    Main limits

    • First-round effects only. Prices, wages and company profits do not respond to the payment or the taxes, apart from the labour and taxpayer responses.
    • Productivity raises GDP and the excess profits tax in proportion. It does not change wages, payment rates or the income distribution. The return on government AI investment is your assumption, as no Australian estimate exists.
    • Migration adds people with the same age and visa mix as the population (the paper's assumption). The six revenue measures stay at their PBO costings unless you change their yield.
    • The unemployment lever moves JobSeeker numbers in proportion and takes the same number of people out of work. It does not move the youth-unemployment trigger.
    • The child payment is modelled at the paper's 18 cent taper only. The cost of assessing it on earlier income, the response to its taper and parents' 16 cent work response come from the household model.
    • Students and Parenting Payment recipients respond to the floor at e61's job-finding elasticity (0.21) whatever the JobSeeker setting. The share of non-lodgers who enrol is an assumption, as no figure exists for an Australian payment that needs enrolment.
    • On the Stage 2 and 3 paths the fund keeps receiving the wealth tax and paying the dividend as on the central path. The stages' own surpluses are held at the debt rate.
    • In a displacement, the macro variants move the six taxes with GDP and profits. GST, dividends and franking and any second-round demand effects are not modelled.
