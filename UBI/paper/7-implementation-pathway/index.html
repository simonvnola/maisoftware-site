7. Implementation pathway

Chapter 7 of 11 of the paper Beyond Work. It lays out the phases and gates from November 2026, the legislation to enact or amend, the agencies responsible and the transition protections. It is for readers who want to know how Stage 1 would be delivered.

Stage 1 can start on 1 July 2028 if Parliament passes the main laws by December 2027 and the payment systems are built and tested in the first half of 2028. A universal payment could follow only under a later Act, introduced after the need test is met, the trial has reported within its work-effort cap and the PBO has certified the funding. The binding safeguards in Table 9.6 then correct Stage 1's funding without further legislation.

Phases and gates

Figure 7.1 draws the seven rollout phases, their owners and 17 numbered gates and milestones; each phase must pass its gate before the next starts.

Legislation to enact or amend

Seven new Acts are needed for Stage 1: the ABI Act, a Basic Income Levy Act, an imposition Act and an assessment Act for each of the two new profits taxes, and an offshore gas royalty Act. No Act sets a universal rate or a dividend. Eight existing Acts are amended, chiefly the Social Security Act 1991, which sets the floor and the Rent Assistance rise.

Responsible agencies

Treasury and the Department of Social Services (DSS) lead, and Services Australia and the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) deliver. JSA and the ABS publish the safeguard indicators, and the PBO, PC and Australian National Audit Office (ANAO) check (Table 7.2).

Interaction with existing payments, tax and superannuation

Stage 1 never reduces a retained payment. Pensions, carer payments and supplements, Commonwealth Rent Assistance (which rises), Remote Area Allowance, the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) and Paid Parental Leave all continue. The lifted payments keep their income tests, including the partner income test, and the activity test, so the floor tapers away as earnings rise. The child payment takes over Family Tax Benefit Parts A and B; Child Support is unchanged.

The tax scale is unchanged, so Stage 1 needs no new withholding schedules. Rent Assistance and the Health Care Card for families hang off FTB Part A, which is abolished, so Table 7.1 re-links them to the child payment. If states count higher payments as income for public housing rents, public tenants lose part of their gain, so Section 9 proposes an agreement on rents and concessions.

Transition protections

Every person affected by Stage 1 keeps these protections.

  1. Every person receiving a payment Stage 1 changes on 30 June 2028, and every later claimant who meets its tests, is paid at least what current rules would pay, including current FTB. This protects payments, not income after housing costs (Table 6.21).
  2. Concession cards and payment-linked entitlements continue for current holders, and the links in Table 7.1 extend them to new families.
  3. Protected top-ups are indexed under current rules, so they do not erode.
  4. No social security debt or protection clawback is raised from matched data unless a human officer has checked it, applying the Royal Commission's lessons on automated decisions [S120].
  5. Every person affected receives a written statement of their floor, child payment or Rent Assistance change before the first payment.

Data and systems

Stage 1 runs on existing payment, tax and myGov systems: payments go to the bank account people already use for Centrelink, and the child payment uses ATO assessed income. Adults without a tax file number, bank account or myGov access, many in remote communities, need assisted claims designed with First Nations organisations (Table 7.2). The A$300m build estimate is weak, because the system must also calculate current-rules entitlements for every protected person and family, so Recommendation 12f asks Services Australia for an agency costing before the Bill.
