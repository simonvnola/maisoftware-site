7. Implementation pathway

Chapter 7 of 10 of the paper Beyond Work. It lays out the phases and gates from November 2026 to 2045-46, the legislation to enact or amend, the agencies responsible and the transition protections. It is for readers who want to know how the proposal would be delivered.

The ABI can start on 1 July 2028 if Parliament passes the main laws by December 2027 and the payment systems are built and tested in the first half of 2028. Stage 2 follows only after the need test is met, a fresh PBO certificate and a vote; Stage 3 would need a later Act setting its rate. The binding safeguards in Table 9.6 can then lower, cap, defer or correct a stage without further legislation.

Phases and gates

Figure 7.1 draws the seven rollout phases, their owners and 17 numbered gates and milestones; each phase must pass its gate before the next starts.

Legislation to enact or amend

Up to ten new Acts are needed: the ABI Act, a Basic Income Levy Act, an imposition Act and an assessment Act for each new tax, and an offshore gas royalty Act. For Part 2, the wealth-tax fund would add the Permanent AI Dividend Fund Act and the wealth tax's two Acts, the surplus fund the Fund Act alone, and the Budget-paid dividend no new Act. Twelve existing Acts are amended, chiefly the Social Security Act 1991, which sets the floor.

Responsible agencies

Treasury and the Department of Social Services (DSS) lead, and Services Australia and the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) deliver. JSA and the ABS publish the safeguard indicators, and the PBO, PC and Australian National Audit Office (ANAO) check (Table 7.2).

Interaction with existing payments, tax and superannuation

The ABI never reduces a retained payment. The DSP top-up, carer payments and supplements, Commonwealth Rent Assistance, Remote Area Allowance, the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) and Paid Parental Leave all continue, and their income tests ignore any dividend (Table 7.1). The lifted payments keep their income tests, including the partner income test, and the activity test, so the floor tapers away as earnings rise. The child payment takes over Family Tax Benefit Parts A and B; Child Support is unchanged.

The tax scale is unchanged, so Stage 1 needs no new withholding schedules. Any dividend is paid once a year, to lodgers at assessment and to non-lodgers by Services Australia once they enrol. The extended Recipient Protection Offset is applied through withholding where Services Australia pays a protected recipient and through assessment for other income. Rent Assistance and the Health Care Card for families hang off FTB Part A, which is abolished, so Table 7.1 re-links them to the child payment. If states count the dividend as income, public tenants lose part of their gain, so Section 9 proposes an agreement on rents and concessions.

Transition protections

Every person moving to the ABI keeps these protections.

  1. Every person receiving a replaced payment on 30 June 2028, and every later claimant who meets its tests, receives at least the net income that current rules would give, including the tax on other income and current FTB.
  2. Concession cards and payment-linked entitlements continue for current holders, and the links in Table 7.1 extend them to new families.
  3. Protected top-ups are indexed under current rules, so they do not erode.
  4. No social security debt or protection clawback is raised from matched data unless a human officer has checked it, applying the Royal Commission's lessons on automated decisions [S120].
  5. Every adult affected receives a written statement of any dividend, floor or top-up, and their tax change before the first payment.

Data and systems

The ABI runs on existing tax and myGov systems: payments go to the bank account people already use for tax refunds or Centrelink, and enrolment uses ATO and Medicare records. Adults without a tax file number, bank account or myGov access, many in remote communities, need assisted enrolment designed with First Nations organisations (Table 7.2), and the Act excludes the dividend from income management. The A$300m build estimate is weak, because the system must also calculate current-rules entitlements for every protected person and family, so Recommendation 12f asks Services Australia for an agency costing before the Bill.
