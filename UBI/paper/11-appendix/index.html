The costings come from a model that applies published Budget, PBO, ATO and ABS figures to a simple representation of the population and its incomes, and the poverty results from a household model built on the same inputs.

Methodology

The fiscal model works in the fourteen steps of Table 11.1, and an interactive simulator published with the supplementary files lets a reader change the main levers, such as the floor, the taper and the five taxes' yield, and watch costs and the levy move [S5]. Its checks reproduce the paper's model; they do not test the model's assumptions.

Table 11.1. The fiscal model in fourteen steps, with the assumptions each uses, 2026-27 to 2045-46 (A$ billion, nominal; per cent; identifiers as in Table 6.55)

Step Method Assumptions and sources
1. Population The ABS estimated resident population of 27.9 million at March 2026 grows at the Centre for Population's rates to 31.5 million in 2035-36, then at 1.1% and 1.0% a year. Temporary visa holders and 0.52 million adults in the waiting period are not eligible B12, E10 [S155, S178, S180, S183, S184]
2. Gross cost Eligible children × the child supplement, less the FTB it replaces, under the child payment's rule; under a dividend option (Section 10), also eligible adults × take-up × the dividend, from 2029-30 (100% of lodgers and 80% of non-lodgers) Tables 6.1 and 10.9
3. The floor Each lifted payment is raised by the ratio of the floor to its single rate, keeping couple relativities and the income tests. JobSeeker-type recipients rise with an elasticity of 0.39, and student and Parenting Payment recipients with e61's job-finding elasticity of 0.21. Applied to PBO costing ECR-2025-3193 (A$88 a day), the method reproduces about 75% of the PBO's 2025-26 cost C4 to C7, D1, D2, F13, F24 [S113, S137]
4. Payments replaced Each program's spending from the Budget and the PBO's medium-term outlook is replaced only up to the adult payment, spread by the share on the full rate; for students, only the living allowance of the recipients the floor lifts (C4a) [S136, S139, S143, S144]
5. Tax and protections A lognormal curve fitted to ATO 2023-24 taxable income bands is taxed under current rules, with the dividend added to taxable income, but not to any income test, and the threshold and LITO unchanged. Protected recipients' other income is inferred from the income tests and protected, with the offset capped at the tax current rules levy on the payment. The child payment is costed from the household model's shares, with a 1.5-year assessment lag and a response to its tapers F6, C8, D4, C9 to C15, F14, E12, E14, F15 [S171]
6. Behaviour and the rate rise Market income falls 16 cents per dollar of net gain from the dividend or the child payment. Taxable income responds to a rise with an elasticity of 0.2 in the standard form ln(M1/M0) = 0.2 × ln((1 − m − t)/(1 − m)), where M is taxable income, m the average marginal rate and t the rise. Each legislated rise is the stage's largest need, rounded up to 0.1 of a point, in whichever of the aggregate and person-by-person forms is larger, with revenue from a per-point method that reproduces PBO costing ECR-2025-2525 [S82, S140, S154]
7. Funding Each PBO costing starts in 2028-29, scaled by nominal GDP, and grows with GDP after the PBO's last year, except the wealth tax (nil), the gas measure (−11.2% a year) and the fuel-credit and coal-and-minerals measures, held flat in real terms (B7); the interaction line follows the PBO's year-by-year profile, grows with GDP after its last year and is charged to the fund, and to its capital after the guarantee lapses. For the wealth-tax fund, the fund and the durable taxes follow Table 10.8; the other options are costed as in Tables 10.2 and 10.5 F1 to F12, F23 [S133]
8. Implementation Build, running, oversight, trial, evaluation and Community Contribution Fund costs are the author's estimates Table 6.2
9. Accounting Underlying cash, as in Budget Paper No. 1, with interest at 4.13% on the change in debt; the fund is treated as the Future Fund is, with its earnings outside the cash balance, a treatment Table 10.14 tests [S61, S139, S168, S169]
10. Paths and scenarios The central path; Stages 2 and 3 on the smallest displacement that meets the need test and on trigger-timed paths; ranges, stress tests and displacement Tables 6.6 to 6.10 and 6.34 to 6.53
11. Distribution Static first-round cash effects per adult and per household, with the revenue measures allocated by decile in each year and in present value over 2028-29 to 2045-46 Tables 6.14 to 6.27; F4-48
12. Net benefit Implementation less administration savings, market income forgone and the excess burden of each financing measure, against the weighted reduction in the household poverty gap Tables 6.11 and 6.13
13. Validation and rounding Mean taxable income is A$77,673 against the ATO's A$78,127; 2026-27 GDP is A$3,093bn against A$3,088bn implied by the PBO; earlier Australian costings range from A$40bn to A$264bn a year. Components are rounded so that rows and totals sum [S139, S171, S216]
14. Reproduction partc.py, partd.py, taper_test.py, partE.py, partF.py, the round scripts (partF_r8.py to partF_r13b.py) the household scripts (household.py to household_r13.py) and the scripts for this revision (optionb_hh.py, optionb_fiscal.py, optionb_report.py and optionb_check.py) regenerate every model table, and make_charts.py every chart; check_arithmetic.py re-adds every fiscal table, recomputes the rate-rise revenue and checks the tables against the model output and the red teams' errors [S5]

Source: author's model (supplementary partF.py and the scripts listed in step 14) [S5].

The household model

The household model turns the income distribution into 40,000 synthetic households a year, so that poverty can be measured on the relative and the anchored line as ACOSS and UNSW measure it [S37]. Household types follow the 2021 Census [S217, S218, S219], incomes are equivalised with the modified OECD scale [S220], and partners' incomes are paired with an assumed correlation of 0.35. Tenure comes from the ABS imputed-rent study [S177, S193], and Rent Assistance follows current rules [S3, S221]. Three factors are calibrated to the line before housing costs, the 14.2% rate after them and the model's child poverty rate of 15.6%, the published figure [S38]; the last pushes synthetic FTB about 25% above the PBO's path. Take-up is 100% (Table 11.2).

Lone persons come out about 9 points too high and people aged 67 and over about 3 points too low. Full-rate pensioners' other income is assumed (Table 6.55), and the relative-line result for people aged 67 and over is sensitive to it: 12.1% to 13.5% across the variants nearest DSS data, with Part 1 at 11.3% to 11.8% (Table 6.23) [S135]. Households relying on JobSeeker, Youth Allowance or Carer Payment come out too poor, so levels for those groups are indicative, though comparisons between packages on the same population are less affected. Tables 10.26 and 10.27 share each decile's revenue measures equally among its adults, which spreads the wealth and superannuation measures more widely than they fall.
