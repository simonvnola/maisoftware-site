About this paper

Beyond Work is a policy paper. It proposes a three-stage Australian Basic Income (ABI), with the first stage starting on 1 July 2028. It was prepared by Mai Software as a submission to Parliament. It is not government policy and has not been adopted by any government.

The paper was revised on 22 October 2026 after an independent adversarial review and nine red-team reviews. The red teams' objections, and the paper's answers, are in chapter 8, Risks, objections and responses.

The paper's own front matter follows.

Chapters

The paper has ten chapters. Each is on its own page. Start with the executive summary if you have ten minutes.

  1. 1. Executive summary
    It gives the whole case in two pages: the proposal, what it costs, who pays, the effect on poverty, the key risks and the decisions Parliament needs to make now.
  2. 2. The case for change
    It sets out why Australia should act now: the pace of artificial intelligence (AI), which jobs are exposed, what the safety net leaves unmet, and the objectives the paper sets.
  3. 3. A picture of 2045
    It describes what Australia could look like in 2045 under the proposal, told through seven people and the aggregate numbers.
  4. 4. What the evidence says
    It reviews what international basic income trials, Australian precedents and the contrary evidence show, and what they cannot tell us.
  5. 5. The proposal
    It gives the design in detail: who is eligible, the rates, the child payment, what is replaced, the Part 2 options, the triggers for later stages and work incentives.
  6. 6. Making the dollars work
    It covers the costings: gross and net cost, the funding stack, the wealth-tax fund, sensitivity and stress tests, and who gains and who loses by household. It is the longest chapter and is for readers who want the numbers behind every claim.
  7. 7. Implementation pathway
    It lays out the phases and gates from November 2026 to 2045-46, the legislation to enact or amend, the agencies responsible and the transition protections.
  8. 8. Risks, objections and responses
    It covers the risk register, the strongest objections and the paper's answers to them, and what the red teams found.
  9. 9. Conclusion and recommendations
    It lists the recommendations, the consultation plan and the monitoring and evaluation plan.
  10. 10. Appendix: methodology, assumptions and sources
    It explains how the model works, describes the household model and the data gaps, and lists the 224 numbered sources.
