Parliament should legislate the three-stage ABI in 2027 and start Stage 1 on 1 July 2028. It should vote on Stage 2 only if the need test is met, and legislate Stage 3 only as an authority with no rate. Part 2, a dividend to every adult, is a later choice after a PBO costing of each option (Recommendation 4); none reduces poverty (Table 5.2c). On the evidence a targeted package of the same cost is stronger on poverty and household reach, while on effective tax rates the two are mixed: higher earners fare better under Part 1, second earners on average under the package. The paper recommends Part 1 as the base for a universal Stage 2, though that base saves at most A$0.05bn to A$0.25bn in expectation. A variant of Part 1 that spends its middle-income supplement on a 75% Rent Assistance rise also has less poverty. The paper keeps the supplement, which a universal Stage 2 pays every child, at 0.32 million more people in poverty and A$2.4bn to A$3.2bn a year now. Parliament could legislate either alternative instead. Part 1 still lifts the lowest payments to the poverty line and ends most estimate debts in family payments. The case for a universal basic income is contingent: on central labour evidence the work-effort cap stops Stage 2 with probability 0.86 to 0.91, and Stage 3 cannot be funded from income tax on central assumptions.

Consultation findings would change the design in these ways.

  1. If First Nations organisations seek community-controlled delivery in remote areas, the Act would provide for it.
  2. If employer groups show that the excess profits tax would cut investment more than the PBO assumed, the stack, and with it the levy and any dividend, would change.
  3. If states and territories identify interactions with concessions or public housing rents, the no-worse-off rule would extend to them.
  4. If unions and the community sector favour a higher floor or the floor-first option, the committee could move along the trade-off in Section 5.
  5. If public submissions show low support for even a Stage 2 tax rise, the Stage 2 authority could lapse with Stage 3's, leaving Stage 1 and any fund.

Each safeguard has a measure, a threshold, an automatic action under the Act and an owner (Table 9.6). For Stage 1 a shortfall in the durable taxes is met by a levy set to the gap once any surpluses kept as debt reduction are drawn. Under a fund option it is met first by a loan in a displacement declared after eight quarters. Under the Act the floor is never paused and any dividend is not cut in cash terms, until, for the wealth-tax fund, the guarantee lapses, though a later Parliament can change both rules. If its ten-year real return falls below 4%, the Minister for Finance tables a review of the payout rate (Table 9.5; Recommendation 5e).

The trial can show how individuals respond in work, health and care to a larger payment (Table 9.7), but not how the economy responds to the taxes, to a payment known to be permanent or to extra demand. A good result is therefore not evidence that Stage 2 or Stage 3 is sound, and the Act requires separate evidence before the Stage 2 vote and any Stage 3 rate.
