Parliament should legislate a targeted floor in 2027 and start it on 1 July 2028: the payment floor at the poverty line, a child payment without a middle-income supplement, and a 75% rise in Rent Assistance. That design, P*, is what the paper's own comparison supports against Part 1, and the paper's choice against the targeted package, weighing poverty against work incentives and fiscal sustainability. It leaves 0.20 million fewer people in poverty than the design the 22 October paper recommended, at the same budget and with A$1.5bn to A$1.8bn a year more net benefit on the complete accounting. If Parliament weights poverty above work incentives, it should legislate the targeted package instead, which leaves about 0.40 million fewer people in poverty than P* but puts more families on very high effective tax rates and needs a larger levy.

Stage 1 is not free and the paper does not say it is. Its first year is borrowed (A$20.7bn, provisional), its monetised net benefit is negative on the complete accounting, its guarantee protects payments rather than income after housing costs, and a levy on market income may come well before its central date of 2043-44: the joint stress test gives a 37% to 59% chance of one before 2034-35 (provisional). Its case is that it cuts poverty after housing costs from 14.2% to 10.5%, with few very high effective tax rates and a smaller levy than the package.

Parliament should also legislate the tests a universal basic income would have to pass, and no rates: the AI need test, the randomised trial with its work-effort cap, and the PBO certificate. On current evidence the pathway will probably never be used. It is insurance against a shock whose probability no one can yet estimate, and it is cheap. No dividend should be legislated now; Section 10 sets out the options and the tests any of them should pass.

Consultation findings would change the design in these ways.

  1. If First Nations organisations seek community-controlled delivery in remote areas, the Act would provide for it.
  2. If employer groups show that the excess profits tax would cut investment more than the PBO assumed, the stack, and with it the levy, would change.
  3. If states and territories identify interactions with concessions or public housing rents, the cash guarantee would extend to them.
  4. If unions and the community sector favour a higher floor or the floor-first option, the committee could move along the trade-off in Section 5.
  5. If public submissions show low support for any universal payment, Parliament could drop the pathway's tests, leaving Stage 1.
  6. If the committee weights poverty above work incentives, it could recommend the targeted package as Stage 1.

The trial can show how individuals respond in work, health and care to a larger payment (Table 9.7), but not how the economy responds to the taxes, to a payment known to be permanent or to extra demand. A good result is therefore not evidence that a universal payment is sound, and the Act requires separate evidence before any Bill setting a universal rate.
