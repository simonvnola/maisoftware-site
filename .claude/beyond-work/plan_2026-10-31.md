## Could a basic income actually work?

A full basic income at an adequate rate cannot work in Australia now, but building one as insurance against mass AI displacement can, at low cost. That is the view of an independent reviewer with no stake in the paper, and the paper's own model agrees with it at every point. The full appraisal, with every claim sourced, is in the project folder as feedback/ubi\_appraisal.md.

| Version | Could it work? | Confidence | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (a) Full basic income at the JobSeeker or pension rate, now | No | About 90% | Gross cost A$440bn–650bn a year, 15–22% of GDP; about 10% of GDP net; income tax 12–31 points higher. Every costing agrees: the paper's, Spies-Butcher and colleagues', the [IMF's](https://www.astrid-online.it/static/upload/imf_/imf_fiscal-monitor-10_2017.pdf), the [OECD's](https://search.oecd.org/els/soc/Basic-Income-Policy-Option-2017.pdf) |

| (c) Basic income as contingent insurance against AI displacement | Yes, in its cheap form | About 75% | Legislate the triggers, build a payment system that can reach every adult, strengthen unemployment support, and pre-cost a revenue plan, without paying anyone until the triggers are met |
| (d) When would (a) become fundable? | Only if at least two conditions hold | — | See below |

### What the evidence shows

- **Trials:** unconditional cash cuts financial stress and improves mental health in the short run, and reduces work only a little. The best rich-country trial, [OpenResearch](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32719/revisions/w32719.rev2.pdf), found participation fell 3.9 points (the paper says 4.2; it must be corrected). No trial has tested what decides the question: paying everyone in a market, raising the taxes to fund it, or keeping it going for good.
- **AI and jobs:** there is no aggregate displacement yet. The [Yale Budget Lab](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/node/1419/pdf) finds the occupational mix not yet changing with AI, and [Danish data](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/working-papers/large-language-models-small-labor-market-effects) rule out effects above 2%. The early warning is entry-level hiring: employment of 22–25-year-olds in the most exposed jobs is [19% below](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf) its less-exposed peers.
- **Growth:** AI receipts fund a pension-rate basic income in the paper's model only if labour productivity grows 3.4% a year. The Productivity Commission's estimate implies about 0.4 points a year.
- **Durability:** universal cash tied to volatile revenue has been cut or eroded wherever it was tried. [Alaska's 2025 dividend](https://www.kmxt.org/alaska-statewide-news/2025-09-19/the-2025-alaska-permanent-fund-dividend-will-be-1-000) was its smallest in real terms; Iran's lost most of its value to inflation; Mongolia's reverted to children only.
- **Cheaper routes:** raising JobSeeker to about 90% of the pension costs [about A$6bn a year](https://research.anu.edu.au/research-innovation-news/increasing-jobseeker-is-long-overdue-heres-how-we-could-do-it-without), about a fiftieth of a full basic income's net cost. [Wage insurance](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr1105) and unemployment insurance that grows more generous automatically when unemployment rises target AI job loss directly.

### When a full basic income would become fundable

At least two of these would need to hold, each measurable and tracked against triggers set in advance:

- labour productivity growth persistently near 3–3.5% a year, with company and resource tax receipts well above forecast;
- the employment-to-population ratio several points below its cyclical path (63.6% now), with long-term unemployment rising among prime-age workers in AI-exposed jobs;
- a sharp fall in the labour share that housing and commodity prices do not explain;
- public consent to a tax take well above the OECD average (Australia collects 29.4% of GDP against 33.9%).

### The strongest case each way

For: if AI cuts demand for labour across the economy, most people would qualify for means-tested payments, so targeting stops saving money and steep tapers fall on nearly everyone. A universal payment is then the simplest way to share the gains, and it is far easier to build before a crisis than during one.

Against: there is no mass displacement yet, a full basic income costs about fifty times what it takes to make existing payments adequate, and most of its money goes to people who are not poor.

### What an honest pro-basic-income paper can and cannot claim

It can claim three things: unconditional cash reliably reduces hardship with only modest falls in work; Australia's payments are inadequate and their tapers steep; and preparing the triggers, payment system and revenue base is cheap insurance against a low-probability, high-cost shock. It cannot claim that trials show a permanent, national, tax-funded basic income works, that AI receipts will pay for one on any mainstream forecast, or that universal dollars cut poverty as well as targeted ones. Option B, set out under The recommendation below, is built on exactly those three claims.

## The recommendation

We propose option B: recommend the targeted design the evidence favours now, and turn the universal stages into a tested, pre-committed pathway. It is the only option under which the recommendation follows from the paper's own results, and it still makes an honest case for basic income.

|  | A. Keep Part 1, fix the wording | B. Targeted Stage 1, conditional pathway (proposed) | C. A neutral options paper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stage 1 | Part 1 as now | P\* (the floor, the child payment without the middle-income supplement, Rent Assistance +75%); the targeted package as Parliament's alternative | Part 1, P\* and the package set side by side, no choice made |

### What B says, in four sentences

Australia should fix the adequacy of income support now with a targeted floor, because that is what cuts poverty most for the money. It should also legislate the tests a universal basic income would have to pass, so the country can move quickly if AI displaces work at scale. It should not legislate a universal rate or a dividend fund yet, because on current evidence neither pays its way. The case for basic income is insurance against a shock whose probability no one can yet estimate, and the paper should say exactly that.

### What must be true before B is published

- P\*'s figures come from one random seed and one calibration, added in round 13. The model must run P\* through the full fiscal path (net cost by year, the five taxes, the levy date, the stress tests) on three seeds and both calibrations before its numbers become headlines.
- If P\* weakens on those runs, the targeted package becomes the recommended Stage 1 and P\* the alternative.
- The PBO should cost P\* (the paper's Recommendation 12a already asks for this).

## Work plan

The fix takes about a week and a half of agent work once Simon chooses an option, with model runs and calculator work running side by side. Two new model results sharpen the case for option B and must go into the paper whichever option is chosen.

### Two new results from the model review

Both are provisional: they come from scratch runs that have not yet been through the arithmetic checker.

- **"No current recipient loses" fails after housing costs.** About 1.11 million recipient households (2.30 million people, a quarter of recipient households) end up worse off once the rent scenario is counted, by about A$930 a year on average. That includes 60% of Disability Support Pension households and 84% of privately renting Age Pension households; no JobSeeker, Youth Allowance or Parenting Payment household is worse off.
- **The levy from 2044-45 is less likely than not.** A first joint stress test, drawing tax yield, behavioural response, AI path and cost overruns together, puts the chance of a levy before 2044-45 at 59%, and by 2034-35 at 45%. A shortfall of about 3% in tax yield moves the first levy to 2032-33.

The model review also found no omitted benefit that rescues Part 1. Everything it can value is worth at most about A$0.3bn a year, while a targeted design matches Part 1's poverty result for about A$22bn a year less. With standard distributional weights on every cash flow (the UK Green Book's), Part 1's net benefit turns positive, at A$41bn–54bn, but the same weights widen the targeted package's lead.

### Steps

1. **Simon decides** (see below). Nothing else starts until then, except step 2's checks, which every option needs.
2. **Model runs** (model agent, 2–3 days).
   - Run P\* and the targeted package through the full fiscal path on three seeds and both calibrations.
   - Confirm the after-housing losers for every design, and test whether P\*'s 75% Rent Assistance rise removes most of them.
   - Rerun the joint stress test with sourced distributions.
   - Give break-even weights on both methods.
   - Allocate the funding taxes to households by decile for the calculator.
   - Cost a phased start (the floor in 2028-29, the child payment in 2029-30) to shrink the A$20.6bn advance.
3. **Rubric version 3** (coordinator, 1 hour). Add a gate: the recommended option is the one the paper's own comparison supports, or the paper names the value judgement that overrides it, quantifies its price and shows why it is worth paying. Add that any "no one loses" claim must hold for income after housing costs. Re-score the 22 October paper on version 3 to confirm it fails, so the new gate is known to bite.
4. **Rewrite** (author, about 40 hours). Retitle; rewrite the summary, Section 5 and the recommendations for option B; rebuild Section 6's central path on the recommended design; move the dividend into its own section; reorder the objections; describe Stage 3 without authorising it. State plainly the A$20.6bn of borrowing, the narrow appraisal, the cash-only guarantee, the after-housing losers and the stress-test odds. Correct OpenResearch's participation fall to 3.9 points.
5. **Calculator** (simulator agent, 4–5 days, alongside steps 2 and 4). The 11 changes in the previous section, quick set first.
6. **Website copy** (Simon). The overview page changes in the previous section; the agents can draft them.
7. **Verification** (1 day).
   - A fresh assessor on rubric version 3.
   - A red team briefed on one question: does the recommendation follow from the results?
   - The same external critique run again against the new site. Stop only when the recommendation-follows gate passes and no criticism of this kind remains.
8. **Republish** the paper document, the calculator and the bundle.

### What Simon decides

1. **The option:** B as proposed, A (keep Part 1 and fix the wording), or C (a neutral options paper).
2. **Under B, the Stage 1 design:** P\* (recommended on current figures), or the targeted package if Parliament should weight poverty above work incentives (9.0% poverty against about 10.1%, but more families on high effective tax rates).
3. **Whether to keep one universal element now.** The model review's smallest universal design the numbers support is a universal child payment. Keeping one costs some of the poverty gain, and the paper would have to price it, as it now prices the middle-income supplement.
4. **The website:** whether "basic income" stays in the headline, and whether to drop the "what the basic income is not" list.
