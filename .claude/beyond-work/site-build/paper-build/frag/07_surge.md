### A surge protocol in a declared displacement

Stage 1 is a means-tested system, and a displacement would hit it as a surge of claims (Section 6, Table 6.54). The Act would therefore set a surge protocol that starts automatically once a displacement is declared, after the need test's youth unemployment leg has been met for eight quarters (Table 9.6), and ends when the declaration lapses. It has four parts.

1. **Automatic enrolment from payroll data.** Employers report every pay event to the ATO through Single Touch Payroll [S_STP]. In a declared displacement the ATO passes to Services Australia the record of each worker whose reported pay has stopped, and Services Australia starts a claim for them, pre-filled, with the worker asked only to confirm it and to declare partner income and assets. Nothing is paid without that confirmation, and no debt is raised from the match without a human decision (Table 7.1). This reaches displaced workers who were employees; the self-employed still claim. The model prices it at A${{ENROL_COST_MODERATE}}bn (moderate) or A${{ENROL_COST_SEVERE}}bn (severe) a year by 2035-36 at the floor, the cost of paying people who would otherwise not claim (Table 6.54, provisional).
2. **A payment system that can reach every adult.** The pathway already requires a system that could pay a universal payment to every adult from ATO and Services Australia records, if a later Act legislated one (Section 5). Building it in Stage 1's build phase, rather than after a trigger, is what makes the protocol work: it is the rail the pre-filled claims run on. Its cost is in the A$300m build estimate, which Recommendation 12f asks Services Australia to cost properly.
3. **Claims-processing capacity.** Services Australia publishes a surge plan before 1 July 2028: the staff and systems it would redeploy, the service standard it would hold to (the share of claims decided within the standard time is a Table 9.5 indicator), and the point at which pre-filled claims would be paid provisionally and reconciled later, as the Coronavirus Supplement period did in 2020. The scale is known: Services Australia finalised 528,000 JobSeeker claims in 2025-26 at a 2023-24 mean of 22 days against a standard of 80% within 16 days, and in 2020 it finalised 1.07 million in eight weeks, at a mean of 10 days, after adding 7,595 staff [S_SA_AR2526, S_DSS_PROC, S_SA_SENATE, S_ANAO_COVID]; the severe scenario's inflow is of that order on top of a normal year. Claims lodged by month in 2020 and the share granted automatically are not published, and Recommendation 15c asks for them.
4. **The gates to a claim.** For the period of the declaration the liquid-assets waiting period, which starts at A$5,500 of liquid assets for a single person and runs up to 13 weeks [S_LAWP], is waived for displaced workers, at an upper-bound cost of A${{WAIVER_COST_MODERATE}}bn (moderate) to A${{WAIVER_COST_SEVERE}}bn (severe) a year during the inflow, less in proportion to the share actually subject to it, which cannot be estimated from published data (Table 6.54, provisional). The partner income test stays, because removing it is the step to a universal payment that the pathway's tests govern; it removes 31.2% of displaced households from any payment on the floor, whose cut-out is A$87,728 a year, and 36.8% under the status quo, whose cut-out is A$2,711.67 a fortnight [S_SA_PARTNER]. A deferral of the assets test on investment property the claimant is not required to sell, for 12 months, a period for Parliament to set, is offered as an option the paper cannot price, because the model holds no asset data (Recommendation 15c).

The protocol does not touch debt. A worker on the floor who cannot pay a mortgage needs a partner's income or the lender's forbearance: on the floor {{DEBT_ABOVE50_FLOOR_MODERATE}},000 displaced mortgage holders in the moderate scenario, and {{DEBT_ABOVE50_FLOOR_SEVERE}},000 in the severe, would pay more than half their remaining household income in repayments (Table 6.55). The paper can only ask APRA and lenders to agree a hardship protocol in advance (Recommendation 15c); it cannot price one.
