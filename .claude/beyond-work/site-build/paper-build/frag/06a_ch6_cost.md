## 6. Making the dollars work

The recommended Stage 1 is paid for by five taxes on large superannuation balances, excess profits, mining, gas and business fuel, with borrowing in its first year. It costs A$62.0bn in 2028-29, the same as Part 1, and A$1,667.6bn over 2028-29 to 2045-46, between 1.71% and 1.86% of GDP in every year (Table 6.1). In 2028-29 the five taxes fall A$20.7bn short, and the Budget borrows the gap, repaid by 30 June 2032. From 2037-38 the taxes fall short each year; surpluses kept from the 2030s fill the gap until a levy on market income from 2043-44 on central yields, or 2044-45 if Rent Assistance grows as the fiscal model's own path assumes (Table 6.6). That date is uncertain: a joint stress test gives a 48% to 74% chance of a levy before 2044-45 and 37% to 59% before 2034-35 (Table 6.9, provisional). Every Stage 1 design has a negative monetised net benefit on the complete accounting, which counts the cost of the taxes and Stage 1's own behavioural costs, and a range spanning zero on the paper's method, which counts the taxes' cost alone; P\* ranks above Part 1 on every reading (Table 6.12). A universal payment at the Age Pension rate cannot be funded from income tax on central assumptions (Tables 6.34 to 6.53). The P\* figures in this chapter are provisional: they rest on the re-costing at Part 1's budget (Section 5) and on how Rent Assistance grows.

### Price basis and status of these figures

All figures are the author's modelling, not a PBO costing, in nominal dollars on an underlying cash basis. They use the 2026-27 Budget's parameters: wages grow 3.5% a year, prices 2.5% and labour productivity 1.2% [S61, S127]. Nominal GDP grows 4.97% a year after 2029-30, matching the PBO's medium-term projections [S139].

The central path is now P\* with no dividend, and its tables are marked "P\*". Many other tables were run on the 22 October design, Part 1, often with the wealth-tax fund's dividend. Each says which design it was run on, and they are kept where the comparison or the sensitivity still applies. The dividend options' own tables are in Section 10.

As in PBO practice, the costing states its behavioural responses [S140]. Earners reduce market income by 16 cents for each dollar of net gain from the child payment, as in the OpenResearch trial [S82]. That figure is the trial's fall in income other than transfers per dollar received, not its fall in participation, so the correction of participation from 4.2 to 3.9 points does not by itself change it; it could not be re-checked against the trial's latest revision, and if it had moved in proportion it would be about 15 cents. The floor adds recipients, including people who leave work and return to payment [S113]. Taxpayers and parents respond to higher rates and tapers with a taxable-income elasticity of 0.2 (Section 11), at all incomes, not only above A$180,000 as the PBO assumes. Effects on wages, prices, employment and goods and services tax (GST) receipts are left out, and interest rates and rents enter only through a scenario.

### The recommended Stage 1: gross and net cost

Including implementation and the income-fall review, P\* costs A$62.0bn in 2028-29 and A$130.8bn in 2045-46 (Table 6.1). It tracks Part 1 closely: A$0.05bn more in 2028-29 by construction and at most A$1.0bn more in any later year, because its Rent Assistance rise follows its maximum rates while rents grow with incomes. If Rent Assistance grows instead on the fiscal model's own path (prices and population), P\* costs up to A$1.5bn a year less than Part 1 by 2045-46.
