### The policy problem

The policy problem has three parts.

1. **The current state.** Australia's income support leaves 14.2% of people, and 76% of people in JobSeeker-reliant households, in poverty after housing costs. It ties security to paid work through means tests and an activity test that bring suspensions and debts, and it pays nothing for the unpaid care on which families depend [S37, S39, S55, S56, S60].
2. **The likely change.** AI is advancing quickly and exposes much of the workforce to change. It has not yet displaced workers across the economy, but the first warning is in entry-level hiring, and the paper's scenarios range up to 1.77 million workers displaced by 2035-36 (Table 6.54). Today's system has no automatic way to share AI's gains if paid work becomes scarcer, and the second-round effects of a displacement, a surge of claims, debts the safety-net rate cannot service and forced sales, would fall on a system built for normal times (Section 6).
3. **The lesson of history.** In the industrial revolution rising output did not reach most people for decades, and people without a floor were pushed into dangerous and degrading work before protections came. A transition managed by counting jobs, rather than by people's living standards, risks repeating that.

The evidence has limits: the latest poverty headcount is for 2022-23, because the ABS did not release its 2023-24 income survey [S45]. No official series has compared productivity with real pay since 2021-22, and Australia has no official AI-adjusted employment projections [S58].
