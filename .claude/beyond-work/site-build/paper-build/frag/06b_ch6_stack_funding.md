### The funding stack

Five durable revenue measures pay for Stage 1, each with a PBO election commitment costing (ECR) prepared for the Australian Greens at the 2025 election [S145, S146, S147, S148, S149]. Each starts in 2028-29, scaled by nominal GDP, except that the gas measure keeps falling (Tables 6.4 and 6.5). After the PBO's last year the fuel-credit and coal-and-minerals measures are held flat in real terms. The fuel-credit measure's base is machinery and large vehicles, which "will still receive fuel tax credits" as light vehicles electrify [S151]. For the minerals measure flat real growth is a conservative assumption: resource export earnings are forecast to ease on lower prices, with thermal coal volumes falling as importers "shift away from thermal coal power generation" [S152]. Growing both with GDP, as earlier drafts did, is the high case: on Part 1's run the levy then starts only in 2045-46 (Table 6.10). All five are first-round: the costings "do not include broader second round effect of this policy" [S146]. A sixth measure, a net wealth tax, appears only under one of the dividend options in Section 10, with the revenue the PBO says it would cost in personal income tax [S133, S150]; Table 6.4 shows it as a memo.

### The recommended Stage 1: the five taxes, the borrowing and the levy

The five taxes do not cover P\*'s first year. In 2028-29 they raise A$41.3bn against a net cost of A$62.0bn, so the Budget borrows A$20.7bn (provisional). The paper calls this what it is: borrowing, repaid with interest at the debt rate (4.13%) from the taxes' later surpluses by 30 June 2032. Net debt is A$20.7bn, A$18.8bn and A$7.7bn above baseline at 30 June 2029, 2030 and 2031. For Part 1 the borrowing was A$20.6bn.

Surpluses are then kept as debt reduction, reaching A$44.9bn at 30 June 2038. From 2037-38 the taxes fall short of net cost each year, the kept surpluses are drawn down, and on central yields a levy on market income meets the gap from 2043-44, at 0.62% of market income in 2044-45 and 2045-46 (Table 6.6; Figure 6.1). Levies total A$36.5bn to 2045-46, against A$25.8bn for Part 1, because P\*'s Rent Assistance rise grows faster than Part 1's supplement. If Rent Assistance instead grows on the fiscal model's own path, the first levy is in 2044-45 and levies total A$19.8bn. The five taxes must yield 100.2% of their costings for no levy before 2044-45, against 99.7% for Part 1, and the joint stress test gives a 37% to 59% chance of a levy before 2034-35 (Table 6.9, provisional). The cash balance is worse than baseline in 7 of the 18 years.

### A phased start to borrow less

Starting the child payment a year after the floor would avoid the borrowing altogether. Table 6.7 costs that phasing: the floor and Rent Assistance +75% from 1 July 2028, and the child payment from 1 July 2029, with families keeping current FTB in 2028-29. Stage 1 then costs A$24.8bn in 2028-29, less than the five taxes raise, so there is no borrowing; the surplus kept carries forward, and on central yields no levy is needed to 2045-46. The price is a year without the child payment, which would have paid families A$26.4bn above the Family Tax Benefit they keep (the budget saves A$37.3bn, which also includes A$7.2bn of timing, behavioural responses and the income-fall review that are not cash to families): most of Stage 1's fall in child poverty is delayed by a year. The paper offers the phased start to Parliament as an option with that price; it does not recommend it.
