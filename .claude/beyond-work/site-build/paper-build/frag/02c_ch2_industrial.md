### The lesson of the industrial revolution

Britain's industrial revolution is the clearest earlier case of a technology that raised output while changing the work people did. Its lesson is about timing and distribution. Output per worker rose for decades before the wages of most workers followed; economic historians call the stagnation of real wages in the first half of the 19th century "Engels' pause" [S_ALLEN], and reconstructions of real earnings find that most working families gained little until about the middle of that century [S_FEINSTEIN]. Over the same decades many people worked long hours in dangerous and degrading conditions in mills and mines, and child labour was widespread and central to the early factory economy [S_HUMPHRIES]. The floor that existed was meant to be harsh. Relief under the Poor Law was local and meagre, and after the Poor Law Amendment Act of 1834 it was offered mainly in the workhouse, on conditions made deliberately worse than the lowest paid work, so that people would take any work instead [S_POORLAW]. Protection came late and had to be fought for. The first effective limits on children's hours in factories came with the Factory Act of 1833, decades after the first factories [S_FACTORY1833]; wider limits on hours, and the social insurance that later shared the risks of industrial work, came later still.

The paper draws three conclusions. Gains in output do not by themselves reach most people's living standards, and can take a generation or more to do so. A floor set on punitive terms pushes people into whatever work remains, on whatever terms, and that is how much of the worst work of the period arose; the paper's floor is set at the poverty line instead. And protections built in the middle of a transition arrive too late for those who bear its costs. The two solutions in Section 5 follow from this: fix the floor now, and build and test the next step before it is needed. These are qualitative lessons. The paper takes no figures from the period, and Britain then had none of the payment, tax and fiscal institutions Australia has now.
