## 5. The proposal

Stage 1 is a targeted floor, recommended because the paper's own comparison favours it. The universal stages are a pathway whose tests are legislated now and whose rates are not. No dividend is recommended.

### Stage 1: a targeted floor (P\*)

P\* has three parts, all from 1 July 2028 (Table 5.1). It lifts working-age payments to a floor at the poverty line. It replaces Family Tax Benefit with a child payment that has no cliffs and no supplement for middle-income families. And it raises the maximum rates of Commonwealth Rent Assistance by 75%. It costs the same as Part 1 in 2028-29 on the fiscal model's basis, A$62.0bn, because the money Part 1 spent on the middle-income supplement pays for the Rent Assistance rise and a slightly smaller supplement. Its full fiscal path is in Section 6.

The variant the 22 October paper reported (A$6,973 a child, with automatic enrolment) was solved against the household model's own costing and cost A$6.2bn more than Part 1 in 2028-29 on the fiscal model's basis. This revision re-costs P\* at Part 1's budget, which sets the child supplement at A$6,750. Re-costed, P\* still has less poverty than Part 1 in every group, on three random seeds and three calibrations of the household model (Table 5.7). Its figures are provisional until the model's last robustness runs are checked.

### Who is eligible

The floor, the child payment and Rent Assistance follow the current payments' residence rules. The child payment keeps Family Tax Benefit's one-year waiting period for new residents [S125], about 56,000 children. Temporary visa holders, including New Zealand citizens on Special Category Visas, are not eligible. Any later universal payment would follow the newly arrived resident's waiting period that applies to JobSeeker Payment, four years for visas granted on or after 1 January 2019 [S123]; about 0.52 million adults are in that period [S124].
