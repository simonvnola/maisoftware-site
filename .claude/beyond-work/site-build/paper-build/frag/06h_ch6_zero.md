### What it would cost to end poverty: an illustration

This section is illustrative. It asks what it would cost to take measured poverty after housing costs to zero in 2028-29, on the paper's household model. None of these designs is recommended. They show the scale of the task, and why the paper chose a floor at the line, the child payment and higher Rent Assistance instead.

Table 6.ZP prices four designs. Each pays households enough to reach the relative line that holds after the payment. The line is half the median income, so it moves when a payment reaches the median.

- **An exact top-up** pays each household in poverty its gap to the line. From current rules that costs {{ZP_A_CUR_STATIC}} a year ({{ZP_A_CUR_STATIC_GDP}} of GDP), paid to households with {{ZP_A_CUR_PAID}} people. It withdraws a dollar of payment for every dollar earned below the line, so work below the line no longer pays. Counting the likely fall in work, it costs {{ZP_A_CUR_TOTAL}} a year ({{ZP_A_CUR_TOTAL_GDP}} of GDP), {{ZP_A_CUR_CENTRAL}} in the central case. On top of P\*, which costs A$62.0bn, the remaining gap is {{ZP_A_STAR_STATIC}} on P\*'s own line, and {{ZP_A_STAR_TOTAL}} with the work response.
- **Ending child poverty alone**, with the same top-up for families with children under 15, costs {{ZP_K_CUR_STATIC}} before the work response and {{ZP_K_CUR_TOTAL}} after it, from current rules. On top of P\* it costs {{ZP_K_STAR_STATIC}} and {{ZP_K_STAR_TOTAL}}. It leaves {{ZP_K_CUR_POOR}} of people in poverty ({{ZP_K_STAR_POOR}} on top of P\*), all of them in households without children under 15.
- **A tapered top-up** that withdraws 50 cents a dollar keeps work worth more. But to reach the line it must pay every household up to about the median, {{ZP_B_CUR_PAID}} people. It costs {{ZP_B_CUR_STATIC}} before the work response and {{ZP_B_CUR_TOTAL}} after it ({{ZP_B_CUR_TOTAL_GDP}} of GDP). On top of P\* it costs {{ZP_B_STAR_STATIC}}, or {{ZP_B_STAR_TOTAL}} with the work response ({{ZP_B_STAR_TOTAL_GDP}} of GDP), because P\*'s higher line offsets most of what P\* already pays.
- **A universal payment** cannot end relative poverty at any affordable rate. Because it is paid to everyone, it raises the median by its own amount, and so the line by half of it. It ends relative poverty only when it equals the whole median, {{ZP_C_LINE}} a week for a single adult after housing costs. That costs {{ZP_C_COST}} a year, {{ZP_C_GDP}} of GDP, before any tax on it or its effect on work. Even then {{ZP_C_POOR}} of people, in households whose housing costs exceed their income, stay below the line.

Measured poverty would not stay at zero in practice, for four reasons.

1. **The line moves.** Any payment that reaches the median raises the relative line. The tapered and universal designs have to chase it.
2. **Some incomes are below zero after housing costs.** {{ZP_NEG_PERSONS}} people live in households with business losses or housing costs above their income. {{ZP_NEG_PAID}} of the exact top-up's cost goes to them and pays their housing costs in full, which would push up rents. A real scheme would cap help with housing, so some of them would stay below the line.
3. **Take-up is never complete.** People have to claim, and the model pays every eligible household.
4. **Incomes are measured with error.** A top-up paid on last year's income misses this year's falls, and surveys record some incomes wrongly.

So these costs are a lower bound on the cost of zero measured poverty, not an achievable target. The paper's targets (Table 2.2) are set at what P\* is modelled to achieve.

The comparison with P\* shows why the paper does not recommend an exact top-up. The exact top-up ends measured poverty for less than P\* costs, because it pays only people below the line and pays exactly their gap. But it does so with a 100% withdrawal rate, so extra work below the line pays nothing. P\*'s floor and tapers are designed to avoid that trap (Table 5.7). The price of that choice is stated plainly: P\* leaves 2.77 million people in poverty whom an exact top-up would lift to the line. Ending child poverty is the cheapest large step the model finds: {{ZP_K_STAR_TOTAL}} a year on top of P\*. A targeted top-up for families with children could be added to P\* if Parliament chose. It would need a lower withdrawal rate than 100%, and so would cost more than the figures here. Section 2 sets the cost of child poverty, {{COP_CHILD_TOTAL}} a year, beside these figures (Table 2.3).
