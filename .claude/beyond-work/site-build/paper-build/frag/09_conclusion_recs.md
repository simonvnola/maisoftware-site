## 9. Conclusion and recommendations

Parliament should legislate a targeted floor in 2027 and start it on 1 July 2028: the payment floor at the poverty line, a child payment without a middle-income supplement, and a 75% rise in Rent Assistance. That design, P\*, is what the paper's own comparison supports against Part 1, and the paper's choice against the targeted package, weighing poverty against work incentives and fiscal sustainability. It leaves 0.20 million fewer people in poverty than the design the 22 October paper recommended, at the same budget and with A$1.5bn to A$1.8bn a year more net benefit. If Parliament weights poverty above work incentives, it should legislate the targeted package instead, which leaves about 0.40 million fewer people in poverty than P\* but puts more families on very high effective tax rates and needs a larger levy.

Stage 1 is not free and the paper does not say it is. Its first year is borrowed (A$20.7bn, provisional), its monetised net benefit is negative on the complete accounting, its guarantee protects payments rather than income after housing costs, and a levy on market income may come well before its central date of {{PSTAR_FIRST_LEVY_CENTRAL}}: the joint stress test gives a 37% to 59% chance of one before 2034-35 (provisional). Its case is that it cuts poverty after housing costs from 14.2% to 10.5%, with few very high effective tax rates and a smaller levy than the package.

Parliament should also legislate the tests a universal basic income would have to pass, and no rates: the AI need test, the randomised trial with its work-effort cap, and the PBO certificate. On current evidence the pathway will probably never be used. It is insurance against a shock whose probability no one can yet estimate, and it is cheap. No dividend should be legislated now; Section 10 sets out the options and the tests any of them should pass.
