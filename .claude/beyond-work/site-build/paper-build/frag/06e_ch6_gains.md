### Who gains and who loses, after the taxes that pay for it

After the five taxes are allocated, 34.2% of adults live in households better off under P\* in 2028-29, or 32.4% by A$5 a week or more, against 32.5% under Part 1 and 43.0% under the targeted package; before the taxes, 65.3% do (Table 6.14, provisional). Of households, 35.4% are better off after the taxes, and 32.6% to 41.4% across the incidence variants; before the taxes, 62.1% gain. Deciles 1 to 7 gain on average after the taxes and deciles 8 to 10 lose (Table 6.15). The taxes fall mostly on the top income decile, which bears 52.9% of them, and the top three deciles bear 88.4% (Table 6.15). The same taxes fund every Stage 1 design, so the designs differ in who gains, not in who pays.

These results come first because a household's gain from Stage 1 means little without its share of the taxes that pay for it. The before-funding figures sit beside them. Table 6.15 gives each income decile's share of the five taxes, the allocation the online calculator now uses. Tables 6.16 to 6.20 give the 22 October paper's results by decile, incidence assumption and payment, run on Part 1 and its six taxes; they are on the fiscal model's individual-income basis, on which 29.0% of adults were better off under Part 1, against 32.5% on the household basis used here.
