What's different

Built for how a family actually plans.

Most trip planners want a finished itinerary on day one. Families don't have one. They have a fortnight of school holidays, a points balance, three tabs open on hotels and a booking confirmation somewhere in an inbox. MaiCation is built around that.

01 · Flexible items

Nothing is forced into a timeline.

A flight, a hotel, a research note, a task and a budget line are all the same kind of thing, moving from idea through held to booked. Two options you can't decide between sit side by side with the cash, the points for the whole family, the taxes and the seats last seen, until one of them wins and the other quietly archives itself.

02 · Observations, not prices

Every price says when it was seen.

A fare or a room rate is a snapshot with a date on it, and every screen says so. Nothing is ever presented as live availability, because on a family budget the difference between “A$2,950 observed 20 July” and “A$2,950” is the difference between a plan and a surprise.

03 · The assistant proposes, you apply

An assistant that can't spend your money.

Ask it anything about the trip, hand it a booking PDF or a fare screenshot, paste a hotel link. It reads the trip through narrow, typed tools and can only propose a change; proposals wait in a queue with Apply and Reject. It says what came from your bookings, what came from the web and when it looked, and what is its own inference.

04 · The email reader

It finds the bookings you already made.

Connect a mailbox with a revocable app password and it scans for confirmations itself, including the ones your partner forwarded from a personal address. Nothing reaches the itinerary until you confirm it in a review queue that shows the sentence each fact came from. Fare alerts and “complete your booking” emails are refused, with the reason.

One booking arriving four times, as confirmation, receipt, forward and TripIt's acknowledgement, merges onto one card. Contradictions are shown, never resolved for you.

05 · The evening before

A memo for tomorrow, at 6pm where you'll be.

Once you're travelling the question becomes “what happens tomorrow, and what will they ask me for?”. The memo answers it: the day's bookings in order with their references, where tonight's bed is, the forecast, a public holiday or a raised travel advisory if there is one, and a leave by time worked back from the first booking's address. It emails itself at 6pm on the clock of the day it covers.

06 · The vault

Passports, from any browser, encrypted.

Passports, visas, insurance and emergency contacts, encrypted at rest and behind a second passphrase on top of your login. Scan a passport and the machine-readable zone is parsed and checked against its own check digits, so the number and expiry are verified rather than guessed. It locks itself after ten minutes.

07 · Sharing that redacts

Share the plan, not the confirmation numbers.

A shared page or an exported HTML file is built from a public view that never contains booking references, loyalty numbers, full names or contact details. Grandparents get the itinerary; nobody gets the passport numbers.

08 · Works in the airport

Saved on the device when the network isn't there.

Every page you've opened is kept on the device and served back when roaming is off, with a plain banner saying you're looking at a saved copy. The budget keeps working offline too and replays its changes when you're back.

Questions

Does it book anything for me?

No, and by design. The assistant can prepare a search with your dates and party and hand you the live site, but it cannot make a booking or spend money. Every price it shows is a snapshot with its observation time, never a hold.

What does the email reader actually read?

It lists only sender, subject and date, reads a body only when the envelope already looks travel-related, stores only travel emails, and keeps nothing but an anonymous fingerprint of everything else so it isn't looked at twice. Every message it looked at is listed, with what it decided and why, and each decision can be overridden.

Which AI does it use, and what does it see?

The assistant runs on Claude, by Anthropic, server-side. It sees the trip you're asking about and the document you hand it, through narrow tools, and treats everything inside a document or a web page as data rather than instructions. Vault contents are never sent to it except the one scan you ask for, and that result is shown to you before anything is saved.

Can grandparents see the plan?

Yes, through a share link or an exported page, both built from a redacted view with no booking references, loyalty numbers, full names or contact details. People who are on the trip can be invited to edit it, and the household can add members who share the vault and the mailbox.

What happens when the pass ends?

Everything you planned stays and stays editable. The assistant, the email reader, document reading and the evening memo pause until the next pass. Nothing is deleted, and nothing renews on its own.
